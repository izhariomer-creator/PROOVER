02The method

How a media audit is built

The same steps the Asserson team did by hand, built once in Proover and run on every article.

How a media audit runs

  1. Ingest everything. Every article, broadcast transcript, podcast and post in the window, including foreign language services.
  2. Test against the outlet's own rules. Editorial guidelines become published checks for accuracy, impartiality and language.
  3. Measure both sides. Each finding is set against how the outlet covered the other side.
  4. Report with receipts. Experts review the flags, and every claim links to its source.

What a finding looks like

Illustrative, not a real finding
Possible breach
"…the figure, reported without attribution, was repeated in the opening line…"
Rule
Accuracy: sourcing of contested figures
Source
Outlet, programme, date, timestamp
Baseline
How comparable figures from the other side were sourced
Review
Confirmed by a human analyst
Why the results hold up
  • Every answer links to the exact sentence
  • Repeatable runs with a full audit log
  • Rules published alongside results
  • Expert validation built into the workflow
  • Both sides measured the same way
  • Export to Excel, with validation data

05Customers and model

Who buys a media audit

Fixed-fee audits first, then reports that keep the outlet under watch.

Who buys it

  • Media watchdogs and advocacy organisations
  • Regulators, broadcaster boards and ombudsmen
  • Parliamentarians and public inquiries
  • Law firms preparing complaints or defamation cases
  • Foundations and donors funding media accountability

What they buy

  • Forensic audit: a full study of one outlet and period, fixed fee, £15–30k
  • Monitoring: ongoing tracking of an outlet or topic, a few £k per report
  • Complaint pack: an evidence bundle ready for a regulator or the outlet itself, a few £k per report
